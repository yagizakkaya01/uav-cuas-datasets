# Overview (Executive Summary)

**Owner:** Mert Gökalp · **Weight:** 5%

This report presents the progress of the UAV Threat Map and Decision Support System, an ESC 491 project carried out by four Computer Engineering students and one Electrical and Electronics Engineering student. Small commercial drones have become a real threat to airports, military sites and other critical facilities. Detecting them is difficult, and once one has been detected, an operator still has to decide quickly how dangerous it is and what to do. The project aims to build a system that turns UAV detections into a live, explainable threat picture of a protected site and recommends a response that the operator can verify against published doctrine.

The report first states the problem and the project's aim and objectives. It then reviews the literature on drone incidents and detection sensors, map data, academic studies and datasets, doctrine, and Turkish counter-drone systems, ending with a comparison that identifies the research gap. It also specifies the functional, non-functional and domain requirements of the system. The research method follows the Design Science Research Methodology with iterative prototyping in five phases: each module is first developed against mock data and public datasets, then integrated on recorded data, and finally tested live on campus. The system design is presented as a five-layer architecture with use case, activity and deployment diagrams. Following the supervisors' guidance, the physical demonstration has been narrowed to a single fixed IP camera, an ESP32-based 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi / wireless communication receiver and a laptop. The report closes with the difficulties faced so far, mainly the changes in hardware scope and the still open choice of pilot region, and with a retrospective on the team's work.
