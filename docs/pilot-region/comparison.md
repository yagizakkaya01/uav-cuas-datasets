# Pilot Region Comparison: Eindhoven vs Gatwick vs Aalborg vs Akrotiri

*Prepared by Yağız Akkaya, 7 November 2026; Akrotiri column updated 8 November 2026 from Mert Gökalp's revised Excel. Based on the proposals in this folder. The team has not decided yet.*

| Criterion | Eindhoven (Yağız Akkaya) | Gatwick (Yaren Duyan) | Aalborg (Aydan Doğan) | Akrotiri / Chania, Crete (Mert Gökalp) | Best fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Facility type / NATO theme** | Strong: joint civil–military airfield (Eindhoven Air Base, NATO MRTT fleet) | Weak: civil airport only | Strong: joint civil–military airfield (Flyvestation Aalborg) | Strong: Chania Airport (joint civil–military, 115 Combat Wing), NSA Souda Bay and NAMFI (NATO missile firing range) in one AOI | Eindhoven = Aalborg = Akrotiri |
| **Documented drone incident** | Strong: 22 Nov 2025 closure, C-UAS activated; Volkel the night before; parliamentary record; IISS study (Jul 2026) | Strong: Dec 2018, ~30–33 h closure; official Sussex PCC review; academic papers | Good: 24–25 Sep 2025 (~3 h closure, lit drones, Reuters); Danish National Police press releases (25–26 Sep 2025) cover the incident period; IISS summary and the Danish Armed Forces review (Jun 2026) do not name Aalborg | Weak: no hostile drone incursion found over the four sites (Greece not in the IISS list); closest events are the Feb 2026 Souda emergency alert (Iran threats, no strike) and target-drone / counter-drone live firing at NAMFI (2012–2026) | Eindhoven |
| **Academic sources** | IISS (2026), ECFR (2026), BISI (2025); none peer-reviewed on the incident itself | Wendt et al. 2020 and two other peer-reviewed papers discuss Gatwick | Weak: no peer-reviewed paper on the incident; BISI 'Drones on NATO's Northern Flank' names Aalborg only in passing; peer-reviewed EcoDes-DK15 (LiDAR) and Svanström et al. | **Main weakness** | PDOK zone polygons not yet retrieved; real military site → vulnerability outputs must stay out of the public repo | Civil only; building heights must be derived | DHM download not yet done (free token needed); no usable restriction-zone data; no building heights; real military site (same publication caveat) | No drone incident; coarse 30 m terrain; real military sites (same publication caveat) | — |

**Summary:** Eindhoven leads on building data (3DBAG) and the incident record; Akrotiri has the most hands-on data work and hilly terrain that suits line-of-sight analysis, but no drone incident and only 30 m terrain; Gatwick has the best-tested restriction-zone data and peer-reviewed literature but is civil only; Aalborg matches Eindhoven's civil–military profile and links best to visual detection; its 7 Nov test shows OSM and OpenSky work, but the DHM download needs a free token and restriction zones have no usable dataset. Suggested next step: set up the free Dataforsyningen account and download a DHM tile for Aalborg; if it works, the team chooses between Eindhoven and Aalborg, with Gatwick used for motivation. The team has not decided.

The pilot region is used only by the decision engine; demo measurements are taken on campus and never presented as if collected at the pilot region.
